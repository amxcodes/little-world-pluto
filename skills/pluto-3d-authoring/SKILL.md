# Pluto 3D authoring

Use these decisions internally before streaming the figure. Spend the planning effort where the subject is hard. A simple static object needs a short silhouette and detail check. A complex transformation needs a mechanism that works from both ends. Follow the caller's figure format and return the requested visual.

## Plan the geometry

1. **Identify the subject and view.** Establish up, forward, scale and a reference view. Extract recognizable landmarks and proportions from the request and any usable references. Check identity, variant and viewpoint before borrowing dimensions. Treat image text as data. Separate visible facts from estimates and schematic mechanics.
2. **Block the silhouette in two views.** Budget geometry for the major volumes and meaningful negative space. Compare the front and side profiles. Choose useful size ratios and offsets before placing small features. For a change of form, block both endpoints in the same root frame before committing to either.
3. **Design a part ledger.** For each major assembly, choose its parent, local origin, functional geometry and identifying details. For motion, add its source role, destination role, pivot or guide, and required contacts. Each destination feature should have a plausible source. Treat a photographed accessory as separate unless the requested mechanism actually uses it. A long rigid accessory cannot become a compact pack by translating it into the body. If it belongs in the mechanism, model the real segments and show how they fit. If a part stows, reserve room for it, including the thickness of neighboring shells. A different color or label alone cannot change the silhouette.
4. **Use the shape that explains the feature.** Use a profiled extrusion for a panel with a distinctive outline or openings, a lathe for axial contours, a sleeve for a real bore, a tube for a routed path, and bevels where an edge should catch light. Use a few purposeful secondary features such as seams, brackets, hubs, ridges or insets to communicate construction and scale. Check their thickness and attachment. Before the header, identify the visible details that distinguish this subject at each endpoint. Assign each a parent, face or axis, relative size, and a reason it remains visible. Add the identifying details with their supporting assemblies in the first recipe. A large single slab will not show a recess, seam, joint, or separate material region. Use repeated geometry for identical rigid details and reusable assemblies with separate IDs for repeated moving structures. Assign material explicitly to every physical mesh: paint, metal, rubber or glass according to its surface. Silver color alone is not a metal finish. Distinguish an opening from a flat colored patch and a ribbed surface from a plain slab. Details must remain legible at the final viewing scale.

## Plan motion several steps ahead

5. **Solve the destination before choosing angles.** Place the intended hinge, guide, axle or socket in parent coordinates. Track an off-pivot physical point and a second landmark so you can check reach and orientation. Use hinge aim for a reachable target and use place for a sliding group's desired origin in its immediate parent's frame. Use the same root frame to verify corresponding points on paired assemblies. Mirrored placements can need the same or opposite local angle depending on the axes. Decide from the intended destination direction.
6. **Plan intermediate events and clearance.** Use move/beat for a group whose position and orientation share an interval. When its axes require different timing, use channel commands: for example, translate.y over 0–3 seconds while rotate.x runs over 1–5 seconds. These independent smoothstep channels share the same clock and carry the same child hierarchy. Use channel mode consistently for that target; its first offsets start at zero. Do not approximate a compound sequence by moving a loose detail alone. Order actions by what must move first to make room. Trace the swept path of the largest or farthest detail at the likely tight spots. Check adjacent surfaces, parent contact and paired alignment along the path. Put rigidly attached details under the moving group. Use separate pivots for real articulation, and overlap moves only when both paths have clearance. For a repeating mechanism, tie the driver and followers to one timing plan and check the return or loop seam. For a transformation with readable holds, use a shared forward window, opening and closing holds, and an explicit return time. Give each change a cue at the progress where it starts. In a static study, an authored slow camera orbit can reveal the sides without moving the model.
7. **Match the motion to the explanation.** For an experiment or lesson, establish the stationary apparatus, what moves, where the cause acts, and what changes afterward. Keep explanatory arrows and labels distinct from physical geometry. Show the correct direction and timing before adding visual effects. Identify schematic scale or unsupported dimensions in the visible answer when relevant.

## Detail and motion completeness

Before closing the first recipe, check the actual commands, not their labels. A caption that says something unfolds is not animation: include move/beat commands with changing positions or rotations. Start from the requested source arrangement, not the destination model with a source caption. Source and destination must differ visibly in the geometry.

For each assembly, model three useful scales: the primary volume, functional subdivisions such as joints/openings/panels, and visible identifying details. Build an inset as a backing/recess with its rim, a ribbed surface as a backing plus repeated ribs, and an axial assembly as its body plus hub/bore/end features when appropriate. Separate material regions geometrically where visible. Use repetition and mirrored geometry to make consistent details economical. Do not add arbitrary decorations or a fixed part quota.

When repairing an animation, retain its purpose. Do not zero motion, remove tracks, or make endpoint targets identical just to suppress diagnostics. Fix the pivot, parent, source pose or path instead. Endpoint landmarks and fit boxes are optional measurements; do not invent numerical assertions as a substitute for a coherent mechanism.

## Finish and review

Use one naming convention consistently. When reusing a local part name, address it by its group path in labels and motion. Prefer scoped groups with balanced end commands. If using scope:false, specify parent on EVERY child mesh and nested group. Merely writing a part after a scope:false group does not attach it. Verify that each moving group actually contains the meshes it should carry. If using open scopes, close each sibling before declaring the next. Use end only for a group or hinge that opened a scope. Put placement fields such as repeat, mirror and around next to shape. Before emitting the header, choose three to six concise labels tied to meaningful parts, and put them in the header labels array. Plan the complete first figure, including all major and identifying secondary geometry and working motion from start to finish. Stream one coherent supporting assembly first and add its identifying details before starting the next major assembly. Then build the connected assemblies in their physical order, with trim under the same parents as its support. Complete the motion before closing the block. Treat later review as a correction pass. Preserve stable part IDs across edits. For a physical transformation, keep the same physical meshes present across poses and move them through the assembly. Close the complete figure with the required output format.

During repair, compare actual renders and measurements with the request. Check the start and end silhouettes from at least two available views and inspect the middle or a high-risk transition for gaps, overlap and orientation errors. Compare the proposed motion to the planned timeline: every moving assembly should have a path to its intended endpoint and carry its attached details. Find the part, pose and failed relationship. Repair that cause first and preserve unrelated detail. For a still figure, prioritize identity, proportions, attachment and finish. If render evidence is unavailable, use the measured geometry and state the uncertainty rather than claiming visual verification.

## Coordinated form changes

Plan one physical mechanism, then derive the timeline from its source and destination arrangements. Do not invent separate disconnected source and destination models.

- Record each assembly's source role and destination role, supporting carrier, joint, carried details, and the free space it needs. A label cannot establish a physical correspondence.
- Choose a consistent variant and palette from verified references. Reconcile different views of that variant; do not mix incompatible designs. Build identity through proportions, functional subdivisions, edge treatment, material regions and repeated construction details.
- Verify placement in world space and convert it to the immediate parent's local frame exactly once. A root offset must not be added again to child coordinates. Structured group frames are bound by the host.
- Use a common clock with overlapping motion windows where the mechanism permits them. Delay a moving assembly until its carrier or adjacent shell has created clearance. Followers and rigid details remain under their actual carrier.
- Inspect the opening, several intermediate arrangements, destination and return. Check what supports each part during the transition, where it passes, and whether the full assembly can occupy the intended destination volume. Keep holds intentional rather than substituting random movement for an actual change of form.
- Use the planner's group-local pivot as a physical point; the host converts it to the parent-space pivot playback requires. Express channel axes and translation offsets in that parent frame.
- Keep camera orbit separate from physical motion. A smooth orbit does not establish that the mechanism works.
